I build machine-learning models for simulation, and I test them the way simulation software is tested.

For sixteen years I worked on simulation software at Ansys, most of them in R&D verification, last as a lead verification engineer. Now I work on what comes next for those tools: surrogate models trained on CFD, reduced-order models of whole flow fields, controllers trained by reinforcement learning inside them, and physics-informed networks and neural operators. Each project below says what I built, what I measured, and which results held up when I ran them again.

Projects

  • A flap controller trained inside a CFD-built surrogateDeep reinforcement learning holds an airfoil's lift through gusts, with the aerodynamics learned from 489 RANS simulations.
  • Eight numbers per flow fieldPOD and a β-VAE compress whole RANS fields; a surrogate on top screens 3,600 airfoils and stands in for the coefficients in the control loop.
  • Physics in the loss: PINNs and a DeepONetA PINN written by hand, the same recipe turned into an inverse problem in DeepXDE and by hand, and a DeepONet that learns a whole family of Darcy problems.
  • One U-Net, two questionsThe same network sharpens turbulent channel-flow fields to a third of bicubic's error on every test plane, and cannot step them in time beyond the mean flow; both results checked against the reference that costs nothing.
  • From modes to a twinPOD reduced-order models of a heated plate, broken on purpose at the edge of their family, then the same basis as a hybrid digital twin of a steel ladle with sensors placed by the modes and a guard calibrated apart.
  • Forecasting a turbulence signalRecurrent networks halve persistence on a near-wall velocity signal and a small Transformer ties it, three seeds each; on the same windows a linear model fitted in 42 ms beats them all, and keeps its lead out to 300 steps ahead.
  • Compress, structure, evolveAutoencoders keep a wake snapshot in ten numbers twice as well as POD on a random split and worse than POD on a split in time; a Koopman autoencoder trained to step those numbers forward loses to DMD even when a multi-step loss and a spectral penalty make it stable.
  • Reading the flow off the wallA 1-D U-Net predicts the near-wall velocity of a turbulent channel from the wall shear stress: 2 to 3 times better than the correct wall law, not a thousand, with ridge regression close behind, nothing beating the mean beyond y+ = 30, and no gain from twice the training.
  • Texture without a meanA diffusion model trained on turbulent channel-flow planes learns the streaks and wall layers of the flow and not its mean profile; measured against held-out fields between a Gaussian baseline and the training ensemble, with a calibrated copying test, and a cosine schedule that fixes the shape of the mean profile and not the rest.
  • SwiftCFD: a CFD surrogate you can tryA U-Net predicts steady laminar flow around a 2-D obstacle in about 50 ms on a CPU, where OpenFOAM takes minutes to hours; R² 0.9974 on 295 test cases, and a Streamlit app on Hugging Face Spaces to draw your own obstacle.
  • A multi-agent legal research pipelinePlanner, executor, grader, replanner and writer agents in LangGraph, running locally on Ollama and ChromaDB, with typed state as the guardrail against invented citations and every run traced in LangSmith.
  • Agentic test automation with PlaywrightA coding agent driven through the Playwright MCP explored a running web app and wrote its end-to-end suite: 197 tests across five browser and device projects, run by GitHub Actions on every push.
